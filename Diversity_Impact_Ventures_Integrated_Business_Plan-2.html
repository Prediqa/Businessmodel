Unit 3 · New

AI-GCC: governing healthcare AI for any organization

Naming note — resolve before external use. Here, GCC means Governance, Capability and Compliance. Unit 2 uses the same three letters for Global Capability Centre. The two meanings are unrelated, and the shared acronym is a foreseeable source of confusion. Consider AI-GRC (a recognized industry term) instead, and confirm the choice with company leadership before external use.

Unlike Unit 2, the buyer is not limited to hospitals: insurers and TPAs, pharmaceutical and life-sciences companies, medical-device and SaMD manufacturers, digital-health companies, and government health programmes all build, buy or deploy healthcare AI and need it governed responsibly.

AI governance rapid assessment — inventory, risk-tier map and gap report

AI-GCC framework implementation — governance charter, model-risk policy, approval workflow

Regulatory & compliance mapping — CDSCO/ABDM/DPDP, FDA SaMD/HIPAA, EU AI Act/MDR, as applicable

Model risk & bias assessment — before and after deployment

Third-party AI due diligence — before a hospital, payer or pharma company buys or licenses a model

Post-deployment surveillance — drift detection, incident reporting, revalidation triggers

Independence and conflicts of interest. Diversity already requires that Prediqa remain optional and separately priced elsewhere. That rule applies with particular force here: an AI-GCC engagement that assesses third-party AI is compromised if the client isn't told Diversity also sells an AI-powered claims product. Disclose commercial interest before accepting a vendor-assessment mandate, and recuse or route conflicted decisions to the managing director.

How we bring impact for our clients

Four different services. One standard: leave the client better off than the engagement found them.

Each unit is judged by a different, concrete outcome — not by activity or hours billed.

Fewer pends, faster cash

Every eligible claim is reconciled against scheme rules and clinical evidence before it leaves the building — catching correctable gaps while they're still correctable, not months later as a deduction.

Prediqa

Capability that outlives the engagement

Clients leave with a trained, independently operating team and their own governed data infrastructure — not a permanent dependency on an outside vendor.

GCC & Digital Transformation

AI you can defend to a regulator or a board

Organizations can show exactly how an AI model was assessed, governed and monitored — turning a compliance question into a documented answer before it becomes a liability.

AI-GCC

Stability first, then improvement

Continuity of patient care is protected from day one, with cash, staffing and safety stabilized before any turnaround initiative is layered on top.

Use cases for all four services

Where each unit earns its place

Illustrative scenarios showing how a client engages each unit — not verified case studies.

Prediqa

A missing implant invoice, caught before submission

A CGHS-empanelled hospital submitting ~300 claims a month runs every discharge through Prediqa before the bill goes out. A surgical claim shows 3 billed ICU days against a transfer record supporting 2, and a missing implant invoice — both flagged and resolved before submission, instead of surfacing as a deduction eight weeks later.

GCC & Digital Transformation

A hospital group builds its own analytics team

A four-facility group wants EMRAM-aligned digital maturity without permanent dependency on an outside vendor. A 6-week assessment and a 6-month build stand up shared infection, readmission and bed-utilization dashboards — with the client's own staff trained to operate and extend them independently.

AI-GCC

A fraud-detection model, governed before it goes live

A third-party administrator is about to deploy a claims-fraud AI model. Before launch, AI-GCC runs a rapid assessment — bias testing across patient groups, DPDP compliance mapping, and a documented risk-tier report the TPA can show its board or a regulator if the model is ever challenged.

Hospital O&M and M&A

A Day 1 plan, ready before the deal closes

An investor evaluating a 150-bed underperforming hospital commissions operational diligence — occupancy, payer mix, receivables quality, clinician dependence — and receives a Day 1 continuity plan and 100-day integration blueprint before signing, not after.
